HKDSE BAFS

Chapter 12: Valuation of Inventory

Comprehensive Summary

1. Types of Inventory

Generally speaking, there are three types of inventory in manufacturing/trading:

  • Finished goods: Goods that are ready for sale. (Note: Only finished goods are covered in the HKDSE BAFS curriculum)
  • Work-in-progress: Goods in the process of production.
  • Raw materials: Materials used in production.

2. Inventory Valuation Method (WAVCO)

While there are several methods (like FIFO and LIFO), the curriculum focuses on the Weighted Average Cost (WAVCO) method.

Weighted average cost per unit =
(Total cost of goods available for sale) ÷ (Total number of units available for sale)

3. Lower of Cost and Net Realisable Value (NRV)

Inventory must be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Compare the cost and NRV item by item:

  • If cost < NRV: Inventory should be valued at cost.
  • If cost > NRV: Inventory should be valued at NRV.

Goods sent on a sale or return basis: Goods should be counted as the supplier's inventory if they are not yet accepted by the customer.

4. Valuation of closing inventory AFTER the reporting period

Often, an inventory count takes place several days after the year-end date. We must work backwards to find the actual year-end value:

Computation of Inventory as at Year-End Amount ($)
Inventory at count date (e.g., 5 Jan) XX
Add: Sales/Returns outwards between Year-End and Count Date (at cost) XX
Less: Purchases/Returns inwards between Year-End and Count Date (at cost) (XX)
Inventory at Year-End (e.g., 31 Dec) XXX

5. Normal vs. Abnormal Inventory Loss

It is common to suffer inventory losses. The accounting treatments are different based on the cause:

A. Normal Inventory Loss

Expected in business operations (e.g., eggs broken during daily delivery). It is treated as part of the cost of goods sold.

  • Identified before inventory valuation: No entry needed.
  • Identified after inventory valuation:
    Dr Profit and loss (cost of goods sold)
    Cr Inventory

B. Abnormal Inventory Loss

Unexpected loss caused by theft, fire, or flooding. It is treated as an expense for the period.

Step 1: Record the loss

  • Identified before valuation:
    Dr Abnormal inventory loss
    Cr Purchases
  • Identified after valuation:
    Dr Abnormal inventory loss
    Cr Profit and loss (cost of goods sold)

Step 2: Record compensation (if covered by insurance)
Dr Cash / Bank / Insurance receivable
Cr Abnormal inventory loss

Step 3: At the end of the accounting period (transfer net loss)
Dr Profit and loss (expenses)
Cr Abnormal inventory loss (net balance)
